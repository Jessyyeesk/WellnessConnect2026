Pit-lane briefing (optional reading before you race)

Blood pressure and hypertension

Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of your arteries. It is written as two numbers in mmHg, for example 120/80. Around 120/80 is healthy. Readings of 140/90 or higher on repeated checks suggest high blood pressure (hypertension).

Hypertension often has no symptoms, so the only way to know is to have it measured. Left untreated it raises the risk of heart attack, stroke and kidney disease.

Care: eat less salt and fewer processed foods, be active (about 150 minutes a week), keep a healthy weight, do not smoke, limit alcohol if you drink, manage stress, sleep well, and take prescribed medicine.

Diabetes

Insulin moves sugar (glucose) from the blood into your cells for energy. In diabetes the body makes too little insulin or cannot use it well, so blood sugar stays too high. Type 2 diabetes is the most common type.

Warning signs: feeling very thirsty, passing urine often, tiredness, blurred vision, wounds that heal slowly, tingling hands or feet, and unexplained weight loss. A simple blood test confirms it.

Care: choose water over sweetened drinks, eat more vegetables and wholegrains, be active, keep a healthy weight, get screened, and take medicine or insulin exactly as prescribed.

Mental wellness: stress, anxiety and depression

Stress is your reaction to pressure, such as exams or deadlines. It usually eases when the demand passes. Anxiety is ongoing, excessive worry or fear, often with a racing heart, restlessness or trouble concentrating, even when nothing dangerous is happening. Depression is a low or empty mood, or loss of interest, lasting two weeks or more, often with changes in sleep, appetite and energy, and feelings of worthlessness.

Coping: slow deep breathing, regular exercise, enough sleep, balanced meals, breaking big tasks into small steps, short breaks, hobbies, limiting caffeine and late-night screens, and talking to someone you trust. Do not use alcohol or other substances to cope. A counsellor or doctor can help, and these conditions are treatable.

Key takeaways

  • Blood pressure and blood sugar can be high without symptoms. Regular checks catch problems early.
  • Less salt, fewer sugary drinks, activity and sleep protect your body.
  • Stress is a reaction to pressure. Anxiety is persistent worry. Depression is a low mood or loss of interest lasting 2 weeks or more.
  • Coping skills: breathing, exercise, sleep, small steps, breaks and talking to someone.
